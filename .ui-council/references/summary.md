# What makes the reference apps good

1. **One shell for every module.** A persistent left sidebar (or rail) and the same header, title style and surfaces carry chat and non-chat screens alike (Codex gallery, T3 settings and usage), so every area feels native to one product.
2. **Hierarchy through restraint.** A near-neutral palette, one quiet accent used only where it means something (status, diff counts, the primary action), and hierarchy carried by size and weight rather than color or decoration.
3. **Numbers lead.** Data screens put the key figure first in large type (T3 usage "88% left"), with bars, timing and deltas as smaller secondary detail.
4. **One row pattern, grouped in titled cards.** Settings-style rows (bold label, one muted line of description, control aligned right) inside sectioned cards with quiet headers scan top to bottom without reading.
5. **Progressive disclosure of machine work.** Tool calls and reasoning collapse to a single muted summary line ("Worked for 8s ›") that expands on demand; the transcript stays readable.
6. **The next action is unmistakable.** Empty states are a direct question plus the composer or primary action; the composer stays anchored at the bottom with model and context controls built into it.
7. **Calm, readable conversation.** User turns in soft cards on the right, assistant text unboxed at a comfortable line length, small muted per-message actions.
8. **Dense but legible metadata.** List rows keep the title dominant and pack status, time and tags into compact muted metadata; long titles get an ellipsis, never a hard clip.
9. **Label what icons and encodings mean.** Icon-only controls, hatched bars and badges need labels or tooltips; contrast of secondary text and off-state controls must stay readable.
10. **No dead ends.** Error, empty and missing states say what happened in words and offer the next step.
